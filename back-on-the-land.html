A year of decay, growth and regeneration

November 2026 · Casha Bowles-Jones

This is my first blog post on the new website. I've been very distant from all things outward facing over the last 12 months: social media, and even my presence on the farm. I've had to take some time to reflect on the last 10 years and, to be honest, to look after myself and my family.

Needless to say, because of all the hard work that's gone into regenerating the land using holistic and organic principles, the land has just kept going. I've always made sure the cows have not needed anything, and their health and resilience, even in the summer we've had, has astounded me.

So I think my reflection is that the land keeps going, as it always does. It reminded me of something I'd been slow to learn: growth needs rest as much as it needs work. I suppose that's what I've been doing for the past 12 months.

I've not been doing nothing, though. In March 2026 I started a journey with a fantastic cohort of people on the Roots to Regeneration programme, run by Caroline Grindrod of Roots of Nature and Clare Hill of Planton Farm. I was really, really lucky to get a bursary place on this absolutely life-changing course, and it's been brilliant.

In 2016 I did a wonderful Holistic Management training, an intensive nine-day course. It gave me the nuts and bolts of what I needed to know, and the confidence to observe natural systems and make decisions. What Roots to Regeneration offers is more. It's about looking to the past: the land's past, your family's past, your community's past, your culture's past. It talks a lot about the story of separation and how we can heal ourselves from it, but we also need to process it first.

We've had the opportunity to see lots of holistic contexts in action on farms, which has been brilliant. I'm a really pragmatic person, so I love to see how things are done elsewhere and bring ideas home in a way that fits our context here.

My context has changed a bit, so I'm coming up with a new one for what's coming in this new future that has been unravelling over the past 12 months. That's been exciting, and daunting as well. There's been a lot of grieving. I've lost a lot, and I've had to find a way to make peace with what's been lost. I don't think that's over yet, and it's still taking up a lot of energy. On my own it would have been a lonely journey – the support I have had has been life-saving.

I'm not quite halfway through the course, and we're moving into all sorts of fantastic things like personal resilience and regenerative leadership. It's opening up so many more routes to learning, connecting us with inspiring changemakers and systems design experts: people who have confidently gone out and done things, reflected on them, and are now sharing what they've learned.

So yes, I'm really inspired, and I want to make sure I have time to put these learnings into action. They're all tools for business growth and personal growth, and I want to make the most of them.

So here's to a winter of learning and growing. I look forward to sharing more with you as it all unfolds.

Casha
